# Client Onboarding SOP

> **SOP = Standard Operating Procedure** — a step-by-step recipe for doing a repeatable task the
> same way every time, so nothing gets forgotten and anyone (future you, or a future hire) can run
> it. This one takes a bookkeeping firm from *"yes, I'll try it"* to *actively using it and giving
> feedback*.
>
> **Stage note:** we're pre-launch with design partners, so onboarding is deliberately **high-touch
> and manual** (one firm at a time). That's a feature — every session teaches us the product.
> Automate this later, once the pattern is proven.

## The goal of onboarding
Not "set up an account." The goal is: **the firm gets a real result (an invoice processed
correctly, faster) and tells us the hours it saved.** That result is what turns a trial into an
advocate — and into the case study that sells the next ten.

---

## Stage 0 — Before the session (prep, 10 min)
- [ ] Confirm the 20-minute slot (video call or in person).
- [ ] Re-read their entry in `prospects.md` (their hook, software, niche).
- [ ] Have the app open (the live demo URL, or a real key set if extracting their invoices).
- [ ] Ask them to have **2–3 of their own recent invoices** to hand.

## Stage 1 — Watch them work first (10–15 min)
> The Dan Martell move: understand before you show. Do NOT open with a demo.
- [ ] Ask them to show you how they currently do invoice entry — screen-share, no editing your tool.
- [ ] Watch for: where it's slow, what they re-key, what annoys them, their software (Xero/QBO/etc).
- [ ] Note the **time** a typical invoice takes them today (your baseline for "hours saved").

## Stage 2 — Run one of THEIR invoices through Zaki Ledger (5–10 min)
- [ ] Upload one of their real invoices.
- [ ] Walk through it together: the confidence scores, the flagged fields, one-click approve.
- [ ] Deliberately correct a field → show it recorded in the ledger → explain it learns for next time.
- [ ] Compare: their manual time vs. review-and-approve time. Say the number out loud.

## Stage 3 — Give access + a simple first task (5 min)
- [ ] Give them access (demo URL for now; a proper account once auth exists).
- [ ] Agree ONE small thing for them to try this week (e.g. "run 5 invoices from Supplier X").
- [ ] Set expectations honestly: it's early, their feedback shapes it, nothing posts without them.
- [ ] Swap best contact (WhatsApp/email) for quick support.

## Stage 4 — First-week check-in (15 min, ~5–7 days later)
- [ ] Ask what they tried, what worked, what broke.
- [ ] Read every correction/override they made — each is a product to-do.
- [ ] Capture the headline: **roughly how many minutes/hours did it save this week?**
- [ ] Update their row in the outreach tracker + log insights in `docs/knowledge-base.md`.

## Stage 5 — Turn a happy user into proof
- [ ] If they're getting value, ask for a short quote + the saved-hours number (→ case study).
- [ ] Ask the referral question: *"Who else do you know who hates invoice entry?"*

---

## Support (while small)
- Founder-led and personal. Reply fast. Every support message = a bug report from the future.
- Keep a running list of issues/requests in the knowledge base; the most-repeated ones become the
  next build priorities.

## When to make this automatic
Only once you've onboarded a handful the manual way and the steps stop changing. Then: a self-serve
signup, a "first invoice" guided flow, and a help doc. Not before — premature automation of a
process you haven't proven just bakes in the wrong steps.

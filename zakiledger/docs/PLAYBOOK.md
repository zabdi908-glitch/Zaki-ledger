# Zaki Ledger — Founder Playbook

> Answers to the four questions that keep a founder up at night, grounded in
> Dan Martell's frameworks + solid B2B SaaS practice, then **translated to our
> exact situation**: a solo UK developer, pre-launch, selling to small
> bookkeeping/accounting firms.
>
> This is a living doc. Update it as reality teaches us more.

---

## TL;DR (the one-line answers)

1. **Pricing** → charge for the *value* (hours saved), not your costs. Start with simple
   volume tiers, anchor to ROI, and raise 5% every 10 customers (Martell's 10‑5‑20).
2. **Cold outreach** → warm first (network + communities), then cold with the
   **4R formula**: Research → Reference → Reward → Request. Short. Curiosity. Test 3×.
3. **First hire** → not yet. Your "first hire" today is *automation + AI*. When revenue
   comes, hire an **assistant first** (never a salesperson first).
4. **First 10 customers** → founder-led sales. Warm intros convert **5–10× better** than
   cold. Aim for 10 *lookalike* firms so you can see a repeatable pattern. Expect months, not weeks.

## 1. How should I price Zaki Ledger?

### The framework (Dan Martell)
- **The Pricing Triangle:** pick a **value metric** — the one quantity that (a) is easy to
  measure, (b) grows as the customer gets more value, and (c) tracks *their* success.
- **The 10‑5‑20 rule:** raise your price **5%** for the next **10** customers; expect ~**20%**
  pushback and hold your nerve when they say yes anyway. Repeat. Firms that re-price ~4×/year
  grow far faster than those who set it once and forget.
- **Value-based, not cost-based:** never price off "what it costs me" or "what competitors charge."
  Price off the value delivered.

### Translated to Zaki Ledger
Our value is brutally quantifiable — that's a gift. The research says accountants lose
**15–20 hrs/week** to the Big Three. Reclaim even **10 hrs/week** at a bookkeeper's ~£25–40
billable/hr = **£1,000–1,600/month of value created**. So any price in the low hundreds is a
no-brainer ROI — the pitch writes itself: *"We give you back £X of your week for £Y."*

**Value metric candidates** (pick the one that scales with *their* value):
- **Invoices/documents processed per month** ← strong: scales with usage + hours saved.
- **Number of the firm's own clients** ← scales with the firm's growth.
- Flat tiers by firm size ← simplest to sell early.

### What to actually do (starting hypothesis — then test)
- Start **simple**: 2–3 monthly tiers by **invoice volume** (e.g. a Starter / Growth / Firm ladder).
- **Anchor to ROI in the pitch**, not to features: "gives you back ~X hours/week."
- **Don't undercharge to win early pilots.** Free *design-partner* pilots are fine (see §4), but
  when you charge, charge for value. Cheap signals "toy," and accountants distrust toys.
- Apply **10‑5‑20**: nudge price up 5% every ~10 signups. If almost nobody pushes back, you're
  still too cheap.
- **Re-price quarterly.** Treat price as a dial you keep testing, not a decision you made once.

> Open question to resolve with real pilots: do UK bookkeepers prefer per-volume or flat monthly?
> We learn this from the first 10 conversations — don't guess it in a spreadsheet.

## 2. How do I do cold outreach that converts?

### The framework (Dan Martell — the 4R cold email)
A high-converting cold email has four parts:
1. **Research** — a personalised opening line proving you looked at *them*.
2. **Reference** — tie to a similar customer / situation / shared experience.
3. **Reward** — give value *before* you ask for anything.
4. **Request** — one clear, small ask.

Plus his habits: **keep it short** (some of his best are ~7 words), **create curiosity**
(leave something out), and **A/B test 3×** with 3 variations — highest reply rate wins.

### Translated to Zaki Ledger
- **Research:** reference their firm, a post they wrote, or the specific pain ("saw you mention
  month-end close"). Our `prospects.md` list is small precisely so we *can* personalise.
- **Reference:** "I built this after reading how bookkeepers lose 8–14 hrs/week to manual entry."
- **Reward:** offer the 20-minute "watch you work + free early version" — value, not a pitch.
- **Request:** one ask — *"Worth a 20-min look?"* Not "buy my product."
- **Human sender:** `Zaki · Zaki Ledger`. People reply to people, not to `info@`.
- **Short + curiosity:** 3–4 sentences, leave them wanting the demo.

### What to actually do
- **Warm before cold.** Both Martell and the data agree: warm intros convert **5–10× better**.
  Start with your network + the communities where accountants already vent (r/Bookkeeping,
  LinkedIn, ICB/AAT groups). The first believer comes from there.
- Then work `prospects.md` in **waves of 8–10** (the domain is new — it must warm up; see the
  SPF/DKIM/DMARC setup).
- **Run 3 variants** of the email, measure reply rate, keep the winner. Reply rate is the signal
  that tells us when the pitch is sharp enough to **scale volume** (this is the honest reason the
  40 is a *learning* batch, not the whole plan).
- **Goal of round one = conversations, not sales.**

## 3. When do I make my first hire?

### The framework (Dan Martell — Buy Back Your Time)
- **The Buyback Principle:** *"Don't hire to grow your business. Hire to buy back your time."*
- **Buyback Rate:** effective hourly rate = income ÷ hours worked; your buyback rate is ~**¼** of
  that. **Delegate anything you could pay someone your buyback rate or less to do.**
- **The Replacement Ladder** (hire in this order): **Assistant → Delivery → Marketing → Sales →
  Leadership.** He explicitly warns: **never hire a salesperson before an admin assistant** —
  scaling sales on a broken foundation just accelerates burnout.

### Translated to Zaki Ledger (the honest version for a pre-revenue solo dev)
- **Right now, your first "hire" is not a person — it's automation and AI.** You're already
  living the Buyback Principle: I (Claude) am buying back your build time; Zaki Ledger will buy
  back your customers' admin time. That *is* the strategy at this stage. Don't hire humans yet.
- **When first revenue lands (~Month 3+):** the first human is an **assistant / ops person**, not
  a salesperson — someone to handle inbox, scheduling, customer onboarding, and support so you can
  stay on product + sales conversations. This is the bottom rung of the ladder.
- **Do NOT hire sales early.** Founder-led sales is the point at this stage (see §4) — you learn
  from every call in a way a hired rep never could. Hiring sales too soon is the classic mistake.

### What to actually do
- Track what's eating your week. The moment admin/support/scheduling reliably steals hours you
  should spend building or selling, that's the trigger for hire #1 (an assistant/VA).
- Everything below your buyback rate → automate it or delegate it. Everything above → that's *your* job.

## 4. How do I get my first 10 customers?

### The framework (Dan Martell + B2B consensus)
- **Founder-led sales.** The founder personally handles all early sales — not because you're
  broke, but because it's the **fastest way to learn**. Every "no" teaches you the pitch.
- **Warm intros >> cold.** 5–10× higher conversion. Mine your network first.
- **Aim for 10 lookalike logos.** Ten paying customers that *resemble each other* — enough to see
  a repeatable pattern in *how* you won them, and enough references to lean on.
- **The growth sequence:**
  - First **10** → direct founder outreach + existing relationships (buying access to *you* + the promise).
  - **10–50** → warm referrals from cohort #1 + targeted cold outreach to that same ICP.
  - **50–100** → early inbound (SEO, community, word of mouth) starts to trickle.
- **Reality check:** founder-led selling is slow and unglamorous. Budget **9–18 months** before
  anything resembling a repeatable motion.

### Translated to Zaki Ledger
- Your ICP is sharp: **small UK bookkeeping/accounting firms drowning in the Big Three.**
- **First 5** → discovery, not selling: *"Can I watch you do invoice entry for 20 min?"* Free early
  version to design partners. One raving user reshapes the product *and* becomes your first reference.
- **Next 5–10** → convert design partners to paid + start `prospects.md` cold waves + ask every
  happy user *"who else hates this?"* (accountants know accountants — tight referral network).
- **Look for lookalikes:** if your first believers are all, say, sole-trader bookkeepers on Xero,
  *that's* your beachhead — go get more of exactly them before widening.

### What to actually do
- Line up the **first 5 discovery calls** from warm contacts + communities (not cold email).
- Turn the strongest into **free design partners** using the MVP we're building.
- Nail the **hours-saved number** with them — that becomes the case study that sells the next ten.
- Only *then* pour on cold volume. Warm first, cold second, inbound third.

## The through-line across all four

Notice they're the same story from four angles:
**talk to real accountants → prove you save them provable hours → let value set the price and
let happy customers bring the next ones → stay founder-led until a repeatable pattern appears →
buy back your own time with automation, not premature hires.**

Zaki Ledger's whole thesis — *buying accountants back their time* — is literally Dan Martell's
core principle pointed at a market. That's not a coincidence; it's the wedge.

### 5.1 — "How to Get Ahead of 99% of People (with AI)"
**What it is:** An AI operating system, not tips: upgrade to paid ChatGPT, build a reusable
**Master Prompt** (who you are, your ICP, tone, constraints), **system prompts** per task,
**Custom Instructions** for default style, iterate in **Canvas**, then freeze repeatable work
into **Custom GPTs**. His line: *get AI doing ~92% of the task; you approve the last 8%.*

**How it benefits Zaki Ledger:** Two ways. (1) *For our own admin* — build a "Zaki Ledger
Outreach GPT" preloaded with the ICP + the 4R formula (§2) + `prospects.md` context so every
cold email drafts in seconds; plus a "Discovery-Call Debrief GPT" and a "Case-Study Writer GPT."
(2) *As product validation* — "AI does 92%, human approves the rest" **is Zaki Ledger's own
human-in-the-loop thesis and confidence-score design.** Martell independently describing the
exact shape of our product is a signal the wedge is right.

**Why:** It's the §3 Buyback Principle made concrete — your first "hire" is these GPTs. They
turn hours of founder admin into minutes, for the price of a coffee.

**Do this:** Build the Outreach GPT before the first cold wave; reuse it every batch of 8–10.

### 5.2 — "How to Actually Make Money Online" (the PSL + trust-first video)
**What it is:** Win attention with the **PSL** content format — **Point** (the exact pain your
buyer feels), **Story** (a real example), **Lesson** (one actionable takeaway) — one short
video/day. And sell by **getting them a result *before* they pay**, which makes you their
trusted adviser; when they're ready, "ask for the order" with a Stripe link already in hand.

**How it benefits Zaki Ledger:** PSL is our **inbound/warming engine** — the missing half of
§2 (which is all outbound). One PSL clip: *Point* = "bookkeepers lose 8–14 hrs/week to manual
entry," *Story* = a firm we watched at month-end, *Lesson* = how they got the hours back. Post
into the exact places accountants gather (r/Bookkeeping, LinkedIn, ICB/AAT groups). And
"result before payment" is **literally our free design-partner motion** (§4): the 20-minute
"watch you work + free extraction" *gives* them a result, earning the trusted-adviser seat.

**Why:** Warm > cold converts 5–10× (§2/§4). PSL content is the cheapest way to manufacture
warmth for a brand-new domain — and steady posting also helps the new domain's sending
reputation alongside SPF/DKIM/DMARC.

**Do this:** When a pilot converts, don't over-engineer checkout — a plain Stripe link, tier
priced per §1. Ship one PSL clip a week from real discovery calls.

### 5.3 — "The Only 4 Skills You Need to Build a $1B Company" (the C's of leverage)
**What it is:** Four forms of leverage — **Code** (software/AI does the work), **Content**
(SOPs/checklists/videos so you teach a thing once), **Capital** (money buys speed), and
**Collaboration** (outcomes through teams/partners). (Martell sometimes extends this to a 5th
C about doing more per hour.) *Time × leverage = output.*

**How it benefits Zaki Ledger:** It tells a solo founder **which levers to pull now vs. later.**
- **Code — pull now:** we already run on it (Claude building the product; GPTs running ops).
- **Content — pull now:** *this repo is the Content lever* — roadmap, playbook, prospects, and
  every discovery-call learning captured as an SOP so nothing is re-figured-out twice.
- **Capital — defer:** pre-revenue; no raising, no spending to scale (matches §3 "not yet").
- **Collaboration — defer to Month 6:** the Xero/QuickBooks ProAdvisor **partner channel** is
  exactly this lever, and the roadmap already parks it at Month 6.

**Why:** The classic solo-founder failure is pulling Capital/Collaboration too early. This
frames Code + Content as the *only* two leverages that pay off pre-traction — which is exactly
where we should spend energy. Cross-ref §3.

### 5.4 — "How to Make Money Like The Top 0.001%"
**What it is:** The wealthy compound **ownership and enterprise value**, not salary — durable
revenue that throws off profit, with **"expansion triggers"** that grow revenue from existing
customers (LTV). Personal money rules: live well below your means, invest in yourself, and
**invest within your competency** (biggest returns come from your own wheelhouse).

**How it benefits Zaki Ledger:** Reframes the goal from "monthly revenue" to **enterprise value
+ LTV expansion** — which is precisely what our roadmap already builds:
- **The correction ledger is an appreciating asset** — a proprietary, per-client data moat that
  compounds. That's enterprise value, not just a feature.
- **Expansion triggers = the Big Three land-and-expand:** land on data entry, expand into
  reconciliation, then the UK compliance-deadline tracker — each one lifts LTV of a firm we
  already serve. Pair with the volume-based pricing in §1 so revenue rises with their usage.
- *"Invest within your competency"* → your edge is accounting-domain depth; go deeper there
  rather than chasing adjacent markets.

**Why:** It justifies the moat-first, narrow-then-expand strategy in hard financial terms:
we're not just saving hours, we're building a compounding data asset with a built-in upsell path.
Cross-ref §1 and the roadmap's Big-Three sequence.

### 5.5 — "Business Is Hard Until You Build These Systems" (the 6-phase scale system)
**What it is:** Scale in order — **(1) Buy back your time → (2) Clarify strategy & offer →
(3) Build a predictable growth engine → (4) Systematize delivery → (5) Install leadership →
(6) Scale culture.** The offer in Phase 2 = the overlap of *what customers pay top dollar for*
× *what you do best* × *what's most profitable*. Phase 3's engine = three channels in parallel:
**inbound, outbound, partners.**

**How it benefits Zaki Ledger:** A sequencing sanity-check that says **we're in Phases 1–3, and
should stay there.**
- **Phase 1 (buy back time):** automation/GPTs — doing it.
- **Phase 2 (offer):** the overlap test confirms **data entry first** — highest willingness-to-pay,
  the chore we automate best, and the clearest ROI. That's the roadmap's "narrow and deep."
- **Phase 3 (3 channels):** we have **outbound** (`prospects.md`); PSL (5.2) supplies the missing
  **inbound**; ProAdvisors (5.3) are the **partners** — a concrete to-do list to complete the engine.
- **Phases 4–6 (systematize/leadership/culture):** explicitly **not yet** — a guardrail against
  building process and org structure before there's traction to justify them.

**Why:** It confirms the roadmap's month-by-month narrowness is correct and names the one gap to
close now: stand up the inbound + partner channels so growth isn't outbound-only.

### The one-line synthesis for us
**Pull the Code + Content leverage now (AI builds the product and runs ops; this repo captures
every lesson as an SOP); win the first believers by giving a result before charging (free design
partners + PSL content); price for value and treat the correction-ledger moat + Big-Three
expansion as compounding enterprise value; and defer Capital, Partners, and org-building until
traction earns them.** It's the same discipline as §1–§4 — *warm before cold, value-based pricing,
buy back time with automation not hires, don't scale prematurely* — now with a concrete content
engine (PSL), a leverage-priority map (the C's), and a phase sequence to check ourselves against.

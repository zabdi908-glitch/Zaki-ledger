# Zaki Ledger — LinkedIn content bank

Built from PLAYBOOK §5.2 (Dan Martell's PSL: **Point → Story → Lesson**, one post a day).
The job of this content, pre-launch: **warm a UK bookkeeping audience and find the first
design partners** — not to sell. Give value first.

## How to run it
- **Cadence:** aim for weekdays (Mon–Fri); weekend posts are optional (lower reach). Batch-write
  on a Sunday so you're never staring at a blank page.
- **Rotate 4 pillars** so it never feels repetitive:
  🔨 Building in public · 💡 Value/tips · 📊 The pain · 🧭 Vision/opinion
- **Soft CTA only ~every 5th post** (design-partner ask). Asking every day reads as needy.
- **First line is everything** — only ~2 lines show before "see more". Every post opens on a hook.
- **Honesty rules (pre-launch):** "I'm building…", no fabricated traction, no dead links. Add the
  domain to posts once it's live.
- **Make it yours:** the strongest posts carry a personal *why*. Where you see `[optional: your
  story]`, drop in a real one line — it roughly doubles engagement.

### Day 2 — 📊 The pain
> 86% of accountants say they're burning out. It's usually not the clients. It's the admin.
>
> The numbers are brutal:
> • Manual data entry: 8–18 hours a week
> • Reconciliation & fixing errors: ~12 hours a week
> • Chasing clients for documents: 5–15 hours a week
>
> That's the work that quietly crowds out the work you actually trained for — the advice, the
> judgement, the client relationships.
>
> Honest question for the bookkeepers here: how many hours a week does data entry alone cost you?
>
> #bookkeeping #accounting #MakingTaxDigital

### Day 3 — 💡 Value / tip
> A small habit that saves hours at month-end:
>
> Stop reconciling in one big monthly mountain. Do it in 15-minute weekly passes instead.
>
> Why it works: little-and-often means fewer transactions to remember, mismatches get caught while
> they're still fresh, and month-end stops being a dreaded all-nighter. The total time is lower and
> the accuracy is higher.
>
> The principle underneath it: consistency beats intensity. (It's also exactly why machines are so
> good at this bit — they never "get to it later".)
>
> What's your month-end survival trick?
>
> #bookkeeping #smallbusiness #MakingTaxDigital

### Day 4 — 🧭 Vision / opinion
> "Just automate it" is the wrong answer for accounting. Here's the right one.
>
> Full automation fails in bookkeeping for one reason: trust. If software silently posts entries,
> one confident mistake becomes a compliance problem — and you'd never know until it's too late.
>
> The fix isn't less AI. It's **visible** AI. Every field it reads should come with a confidence
> score: the stuff it's sure about, it fills in; the stuff it's unsure about, it flags to a human.
>
> Not a black box. A colleague that shows its working and asks when it's not sure.
>
> Would you trust AI with your books if it showed you how confident it was on every line?
>
> #AI #accounting #bookkeeping #futureofwork

### Day 6 — 📊 The pain (relatable, optional weekend)
> It's 9pm. The kids are finally in bed. And you're back at the laptop… keying in invoices.
>
> This is the bit nobody warns you about when you start a bookkeeping practice. The clients, the
> deadlines, the growth — fine. It's the invisible hours of data entry that eat the evenings.
>
> It shouldn't be this way. The repetitive typing is exactly the kind of work software should have
> taken off your plate years ago — so your evenings go back to being yours.
>
> That's the whole reason I'm building what I'm building.
>
> #bookkeeping #smallbusiness #accounting

### Day 7 — 🔨 Building in public (week-1 reflection, optional weekend)
> One week of building Zaki Ledger in public. A few things I've learned already:
>
> 1. Bookkeepers don't want to be replaced — they want the boring 80% taken off their plate.
> 2. Trust is the whole game. "It's accurate" isn't enough; people need to *see* why to believe it.
> 3. The audit trail isn't a feature to bolt on later — it has to be there from invoice #1.
>
> So that's what I built first: every correction logged, every entry approved by a human, every
> field scored for confidence.
>
> More next week. If you're a bookkeeper and any of this rings true, I'd genuinely love to hear
> what your biggest time-sink is.
>
> #buildinpublic #bookkeeping #accounting
